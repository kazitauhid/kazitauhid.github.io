Summary

Endurance is the chief constraint on multirotors assigned to persistent tasks, yet conventional endurance models treat the aircraft as a purely aerodynamic–propulsive system and ignore the constant electrical load drawn by onboard computers, routers and sensors. For a swarm-hub aircraft that carries an always-on networking and compute stack, that demand can rival the power needed to hover.

This solo paper builds a transparent, mission-phase energy model for a 7.2 kg octocopter acting as the airborne hub of a drone swarm. It couples a bench-measured 45 W avionics load with an analytical quadratic drag–speed relation, exposing every assumption so others can reproduce and stress-test it. In calm air, hovering dominates — about 79% of mission energy — making the always-on avionics the primary limit on station time; as headwind rises, airframe drag takes over. The model turns networking duty cycle into a tunable parameter, enabling reserve policies that protect link reliability without wasting flight time.

Highlights
  • A transparent, reproducible mission-phase breakdown of where a hub aircraft's energy actually goes.
  • A compact mapping from wind environment to expected endurance, suitable for pre-flight and contingency planning.
  • Treats radio/compute activity as a tunable endurance cost operators can manage.
