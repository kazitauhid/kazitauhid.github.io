A Detailed Analysis of Self-Healing Ceramic-Matrix Composites for Aerospace Applications

Hussain, K. T. M., Rafi, M. T. A., Mahin, M. A., & Barua, R.

Summary

Ceramic-matrix composites (CMCs) are prized in aerospace for high-temperature performance and low density, but they are inherently brittle: micro-cracks from mechanical loading, thermal cycling and environmental exposure can degrade properties or cause catastrophic failure. Self-healing CMCs, inspired by biological repair, offer a route to autonomously seal such damage and extend component life.

This review consolidates recent advances in self-healing CMCs, organising them by intrinsic mechanisms (healing agents embedded in the matrix) and extrinsic mechanisms (surface-applied agents), alongside microcapsule, vascular and oxidation-stimulated crack-sealing systems that can restore over 90% of original strength. It compares healing strategies against reinforcement choices, emphasises the need for better healing efficiency and reliability, and highlights the open research gaps standing between current results and practical aerospace deployment.

Highlights
  • Organises self-healing CMC mechanisms into intrinsic vs. extrinsic, with healing agents and systems compared.
  • Surveys microcapsule, vascular and oxidation-stimulated crack-sealing approaches restoring >90% strength.
  • Maps the open research gaps blocking practical high-temperature aerospace use.
