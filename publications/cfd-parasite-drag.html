CFD-Derived Parasite-Drag Law of an Octocopter Airframe with Endurance Implications

Hussain, K. T. M., Kabir, M., Talukder, I., & Atul, F. R.

Summary

Octocopter designers lack a simple, reliable way to estimate airframe drag — the quantity that ultimately sets cruise power and endurance. This study isolates airframe-only parasite drag (rotors excluded) for a 7.2 kg octocopter using CFD across 1–15 m/s, with a k–ω SST turbulence closure and a verified numerical setup.

The extracted forces collapse onto a single airspeed-based quadratic law, D = a·V², which maps directly to a cubic electrical-power model P = P₀ + k·V³ and a closed-form range-optimal cruise speed. Flow analysis shows the drag rise at higher speeds is driven by expanding separated regions at the arm–hub and landing-gear junctions, and that simple geometric fixes — rounding sharp corners and refining landing-gear profiles — can meaningfully cut drag.

Highlights
  • A portable, single-parameter parasite-drag law usable for design and mission planning without re-running CFD.
  • A closed-form range-optimal cruise speed derived from the drag law.
  • Identifies arm–hub and landing-gear junctions as the dominant drag sources.
