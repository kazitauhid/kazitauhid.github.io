Feature-Gated Robust Fusion for UAV Wildfire Classification under Modality Failure

Summary

UAV wildfire perception usually pairs an RGB camera with a thermal (infrared) sensor, but real flights routinely leave one stream missing or unreliable — through sensor faults, cabling failures, recalibration drift, motion blur, smoke, or low light. Standard late-fusion classifiers degrade sharply under these conditions, and the larger backbones that recover robustness do so only at a parameter and latency cost a small UAV cannot afford.

Feature-Gated Robust Fusion (FGRF) is a compact 16,909-parameter RGB–thermal classifier built for exactly this regime. It combines a three-view consistency objective (both-present, RGB-missing, thermal-missing) with a norm-based scalar gate and learnable missing-modality embeddings that substitute for an unreliable branch. On the FLAME 2 dataset it reaches 95.25% absent-modality mean accuracy — a 13.7-point gain over a compact masked baseline — and runs at 6.04 ms per decision (35.4 mJ) on an NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano with TensorRT.

Highlights
  • A norm-based reliability gate (just four learnable scalars) that selectively suppresses corrupted features such as low-light, defocus and motion blur.
  • A three-view training objective with stop-gradient consistency that regularises missing-modality predictions toward the both-present case.
  • A full edge benchmark on Jetson Orin Nano reporting end-to-end frame-pair-to-decision latency, FPS and energy.
