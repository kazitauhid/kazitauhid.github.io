GNSS-Independent Navigation and Obstacle Avoidance for UAVs Using a Compact 360° Scanning Head

Redwan, M. N., Islam, J., & Hussain, K. T. M.
IEEE Int. WIE Conf. on Electrical & Computer Engineering (WIECON-ECE 2025) · Cox's Bazar, Bangladesh

Summary

Many real UAV missions must operate where GNSS is unreliable — under multipath, jamming, or in confined spaces. This paper develops a GNSS-independent guidance, navigation and collision-avoidance stack that pairs SLAM with an omnidirectional obstacle sensor built from a compact 360° scanning head: a single beam rotated by a stepper and slip ring with Hall indexing, whose detections drive small roll/pitch overrides via a distance-to-PWM rule with a 0.70 m safety radius.

The system is tested in two failure-stressing settings — a reflective-wall arena that exposes specular and multipath effects, and a city-canyon scene that exercises drift and loop-closure recovery. LiDAR ranging errors stayed typically below 10% in the arena (versus over 30% for ultrasound), and loop closures pulled trajectories back to the street grid, keeping drift bounded over multi-block runs — showing a lightweight, compute-efficient pipeline can map and avoid obstacles without GNSS.

Highlights
  • A compact 360° scanning head giving omnidirectional ranging from a single beam.
  • SLAM-based loop closure that bounds drift over multi-block GNSS-denied runs.
  • A simple distance-to-PWM avoidance rule enforcing a 0.70 m safety radius.
