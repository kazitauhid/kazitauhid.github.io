Comparative Study of Sequencing and Control for an Aircraft Armament Release Mechanism

Sifat, M. S. A., Hussain, K. T. M., & Reza, S.
IEEE Int. Conf. on Robotics, Automation, AI & IoT (RAAICON 2025) · Dhaka, Bangladesh

Summary

End-to-end command-to-separation timing governs both the safety and performance of aircraft armament release, yet it is seldom reported. This paper implements a latency-aware supervisory architecture in which a Raspberry Pi sequencer issues mode-dependent commands (Auto, Manual, failsafe Jettison) and an Arduino actuator layer enforces positive-separation confirmation, diverting to jettison on a fault.

Two representative mechanisms — a suspension latch and a geared claw — were designed in SolidWorks, 3D-printed, and exercised on a five-station bench with inert articles under standardised, controller-side time-stamped metrics. The suspension mechanism consistently outperformed the claw across all modes, cutting actuation and release time by roughly 0.45 s (about 40%), which widens the sequencing margin and supports tighter inter-station spacing while preserving the shared failsafe behaviour.

Highlights
  • A latency-aware supervisory stack (Raspberry Pi sequencer + Arduino actuator) with positive-separation confirmation.
  • A head-to-head comparison of suspension-latch vs. geared-claw release under Auto/Manual/Jettison modes.
  • Standardised, time-stamped actuation/release/activation metrics on a five-station bench.
