Ground-Station-Free Multi-UAV Formation with an Airborne Wi-Fi Hub and Distributed Avionics

Hussain, K. T. M., Talukder, I., Atul, F. R., Rahman, M. R., Shamim, A., & Rahman, M. M.

Summary

Fieldable multirotor swarms usually depend on a ground link for coordination, which caps both range and robustness. This paper removes the ground station entirely: an airborne master–slave network pairs an octocopter that serves as the Wi-Fi access point and timing anchor with follower quadcopters that receive compact formation cues in a local East-North-Up frame and track them onboard.

Validated with two followers in AirSim and in outdoor flight, the system holds sub-meter radial RMSE on a 5 m circular orbit and shows bounded, low-overshoot responses to ±3 m formation steps. In-flight networking gives roughly 200 ms round-trip delay, and latency-injection trials quantify how tracking error grows with RTT. A lightweight onboard person-counter reaches an F1 up to 0.81 across scenes — together showing that a bandwidth-efficient airborne hub can deliver reliable, low-latency multi-UAV coordination with no ground station.

Highlights
  • A master–slave controller in a shared ENU frame achieving sub-meter radial error on a 5 m orbit.
  • Formation expand/contract cues (±3 m) executed with small, repeatable overshoot.
  • A lightweight onboard detector that transmits only an integer person count.
